Consentia

Informed consent and e-signatures on a business's own documents.

Every signer or grantor verifies their identity by email and by SMS. When the request is complete, the PDF is sealed with verifiable evidence and stored in the business's own cloud; the platform does not keep their personal data.

What it is

Consentia lets a small business (a clinic, a beauty or tattoo studio, a foundation, a school) ask its clients for informed consent or for a signature on a document, without changing its software or its cloud. The business uploads its own PDF; each signer or grantor receives a personal link and confirms their identity with a code; at the end, the sealed document is stored in the business's Google Drive or OneDrive, with a QR code that lets anyone verify it has not changed.

Who uses it and what each one sees

  • The business: sees only its own requests, templates and history. It connects its cloud, creates requests, resends or corrects the details of a signer or grantor who has not responded yet, and retries deliveries.
  • The signer (signature mode): needs no account. They come in through their personal link and see only their own data, never the other signers'.
  • The grantor (consent mode): needs no account either. They come in through their personal link and see their own details, the context, the document and the clauses.
  • Platform team: administrators and analysts with granular permissions (businesses, audit, requests, catalogs and indicators). They never see the personal data of signers or grantors.

The request

  • Contents: the signers or the grantor (an individual, someone acting through their legal representative, or a company through an authorized person) with their ID document validated; the document or template; the clauses or fields; the context, which is required and gets sealed; and the expiry.
  • Statuses: pending → opened → awaiting signatures (signature mode with several people only) → sealing → completed. It can also expire or be cancelled, and it shows as "paused" if the business's cloud is disconnected.
  • What is kept: record numbers, digital fingerprints and timestamps, with no personal data. The data of signers and grantors lives only while the request is open.
  • Verifiable: the sealed PDF from either mode carries a QR code that opens a page showing the evidence without personal data. Dropping the PDF on it lets the browser check, on the user's own device and without uploading it, that the document has not changed.

How it works

  1. Business sign-up

    Individual or company details and the data-processing authorizations, confirmed with a code sent by email; the record is kept.

  2. Connect its cloud

    Google or Microsoft, with the narrowest permission: the app only sees what it created itself. The template and history folders are created.

  3. Upload the PDF

    It goes straight to the business's cloud. Encrypted or damaged PDFs are rejected.

  4. Create the request

    The signers or the grantor, the document or template, the fields (signature) or the clauses (consent), the context and the expiry. The link is sent from the platform's email on the business's behalf, and is never shown to the business.

  5. Sign or consent

    • Signature: each signer reviews the document, fills in their fields and draws their signature.
    • Consent: the grantor reads the context and the document and ticks "I have read and accept" for the clauses they accept; the code can only be requested once every mandatory clause is accepted. No signature is drawn.
    • In both cases they confirm with a code sent by SMS.

  6. Sealing, when the request is complete

    The PDF is assembled with its evidence and stored in the business's cloud, logged in its history, a copy is sent to every signer or to the grantor from the business's own email, and the temporary personal data is deleted.

What the system handles on its own

  • Incomplete delivery: retried without charging again.
  • Business cloud disconnected: requests are paused and their deadline is extended on reconnection.
  • Voluntary disconnection of the cloud: it first shows which requests would be cancelled.
  • Expired request: closed and its data deleted.
  • Usage: each request uses prepaid credits; the business places a purchase order and the administrators activate it once payment is confirmed.

Screens

  • Public: home, sign-up, sign-in with a code, signing, consent and verification.
  • For the business: dashboard (getting started, pending requests, pending deliveries and history); its cloud (connect, test, repair and disconnect); signature templates, with the field editor on the PDF; consent templates; create request; credits, profile and settings.
  • For the platform: indicators, audit, businesses, analysts, requests, finance and catalogs.

Controls

  • Access: each business sees only its own data, enforced by rules in the database, and every server function checks again who is calling. Sensitive tables are touched only by the server.
  • Data minimization: the data of signers and grantors lives only while the request is open; documents are never stored in Consentia; the history with names lives in the business's cloud; codes and the IP address used to limit abuse are stored hashed, never in clear text.
  • Evidence: a digital fingerprint (SHA-256) per signer or per clause and a global fingerprint printed on every page; an evidence sheet with identity verified through two channels, timestamps, IP address and the QR code; and the exact inputs of each fingerprint attached inside the PDF so it can be recomputed.
  • Non-repudiation: the invitation and the code go out through the platform's channels, not the business's, since the business benefits from the signature or the consent.
  • Audit: an immutable log; the client can only read it.
  • Legal basis: Colombian data-protection and e-signature law. Consentia is the controller for its clients' data and the processor for the data of signers and grantors.
  • Change control: the same checks on every commit and in continuous integration, automated tests, SQL harnesses that roll themselves back, and a check that the published schema still matches production.

Integrations

  • The business's Google Workspace or Microsoft 365: cloud storage and email.
  • Platform email: invitations, codes and notices.
  • SMS provider: codes.
  • Telegram: operations.
  • Cloudflare: website and evidence archive.
  • Supabase: database and server.
  • GitHub Actions: checks and watchdog.

Operational details

  • SMS code: 8 digits, valid for 5 minutes, 5 attempts, with limits per person and per business.
  • Request: expires between 1 and 120 hours; 1 to 50 clauses or 1 to 100 fields.
  • Scheduled jobs: expiry every 15 minutes; idle-session revocation every 5 minutes; monitor hourly; daily evidence export.
  • Session: closed after 30 minutes of inactivity. Watchdog: 3 times an hour. Public verification: rate-limited per minute.
  • Retention: fingerprints, record numbers and the audit log are kept for 10 years.
