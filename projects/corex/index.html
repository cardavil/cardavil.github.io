Corex

Sports management for coaches: they assess their athletes with ISAK anthropometry and prescribe training routines and nutrition plans.

It calculates body composition every time an assessment is saved, prepares an optional AI-written narrative report that the coach reviews before publishing it, and shows each athlete only their own data. It runs on the web and in an Android app.

Status
In production · in use
Period
Apr 2026 – present
Role
Independent project, built for friends’ businesses

What it is

Corex is a web platform for sports management, wrapped in an Android app.

  • The coach assesses their athletes with ISAK anthropometry, the international body-measurement protocol, and the system calculates the results on save.
  • For each assessment, the coach can generate an AI-written narrative report, which they review before publishing it.
  • The coach prescribes training routines and nutrition plans.
  • The athlete sees their own data.
  • Administrators approve access, reassign coaches and oversee operations.

Who uses it and what each one sees

  • Athlete: sees a home screen with their latest assessment (BMI, body-fat % and muscle mass), their active routine and their plan with calories and macronutrients. Sees their assessments, and each report only once their coach publishes it. Edits their basic details, but not the coach’s notes, and can request a change of coach.
  • Coach: sees only their active athletes, and creates assessments, reports, routines and plans only for them; when the relationship ends, they keep read access to what they created. Invites athletes, who join as active, and invites coaches, who stay pending approval. Approves people who sign up choosing them, deactivates athletes and requests coach changes.
  • Analyst: starts with minimal access; the administrator grants read or write permissions per module (athletes, assessments, routines, nutrition, activity and catalogs). Cannot create assessments, routines or plans. With write permission, edits athletes and resolves coach changes, except the ones they requested.
  • Administrator: full control. Approves coaches, assigns roles (without being able to remove their own), grants analyst permissions and reassigns coaches. The only one who can take over an athlete’s edit lock, and sees Indicators, Activity, Access and Catalogs.
  • One person can hold several roles and chooses which one to work in. Switching roles can only reduce permissions, never expand them.

What it records

  • ISAK assessment (the core object): date, anthropometrist, sport, age, sex, basic measurements, skinfolds, girths and breadths, with the raw readings. On save, the server calculates BMI, waist-to-hip and waist-to-height ratios with their risk level, skinfold sums, body-fat %, muscle, bone, fat and residual mass, the Heath-Carter somatotype and the proportionality profile. It can be edited by its author, while they remain the active coach, or by the administrator.
  • ISAK report: one per assessment, with seven sections (Summary, Body composition, Somatotype and proportionality, Risk and health, Impact on your routine, Impact on your nutrition, and Conclusions and next steps) plus the coach’s notes. Statuses: generating → ready or error; publishing is separate. Regenerating it replaces the previous one and unpublishes it.
  • Routine: goal, level, days per week, time of day, location and equipment; for each day, blocks by muscle group, each with exercises that have sets, reps, weight, rest, technique, tips and a video. Statuses: active or inactive.
  • Nutrition plan: calories, protein, carbohydrates, fat, hydration, an adjustment for training days, restrictions and preferences, and a weekly menu. Statuses: active or inactive, with a single active plan per athlete: creating a new one deactivates the previous one.
  • Person: identity with demographic, health, participation, sports (including the coach’s notes) and professional (including the coach’s ISAK level) profiles. Statuses: pending → active or rejected; active → inactive. Only an active account can sign in.
  • Coach–athlete relationship: only one current relationship per athlete, with history.
  • Coach-change request: pending → approved, rejected or cancelled; one pending request per athlete.
  • Catalogs: general lists, foods with macronutrients per serving, and exercises by muscle group.

How it works

  1. Sign-in

    With a code sent by email. The response is the same whether or not the account exists: only an active account gets the code, and a pending, rejected or inactive one gets an email explaining its status instead.

  2. Adding an athlete

    By invitation from the coach, joining as active with the relationship in place; or by public sign-up, choosing a coach and accepting the data-processing terms, staying pending until their coach or the administrator approves them. They then complete their profile step by step and accept the consent.

  3. Adding a coach

    Through public sign-up or an invitation from another coach, the new coach stays pending and the administrators are notified; if an administrator sends the invitation, the coach joins as active.

  4. ISAK assessment

    • The coach picks the athlete; age, sex and sport are prefilled, and the screen shows where each value came from.
    • The coach enters the measurements, and the system asks for another reading when two do not match.
    • The first change takes the athlete’s edit lock, and the screen keeps saving as a draft.
    • On save, the server validates, checks access and the lock, and calculates.
    • Results are shown in charts (composition, risk, somatochart and proportionality), and two assessments can be compared with their differences and progress over time.

  5. AI report

    • The coach opens “Generate report” and ticks the box authorizing AI-assisted interpretation, which is unticked by default.
    • Without the box ticked, the report contains only data and charts.
    • With it, the engine works in the background and the screen describes in words which stage it has reached.
    • The coach reviews the report and clicks “Publish to user”; only then does the athlete see it. It can be printed or saved as a PDF.

  6. Routine

    A step-by-step wizard: athlete, settings, days with exercises from the catalog, and summary. Afterwards it can be edited day by day, copied to another athlete or deactivated, and it shows statistics: exercises, sets, reps, total load, estimated time and muscles worked.

  7. Nutrition plan

    The system estimates resting energy expenditure (using fat-free mass from the latest ISAK assessment, if there is one) and total expenditure based on activity, and proposes calories and macronutrients for the goal. The coach builds the weekly menu, with the adjustment for training days. The server reads body-fat % from the database, not from what the screen sends.

  8. Coach change

    • Requested by the athlete or their coach, with a reason.
    • Resolved by the administrator or an analyst with permission, never by the person who requested it.
    • Once approved, the relationship changes in a single operation and everyone affected is notified by email.
    • The administrator can also reassign directly.

  9. Catalogs

    Create, edit, activate or deactivate; import from CSV and export to CSV or Excel.

Screens

  • Coach home: active athletes, assessments, active routines and plans; shortcuts and recent people. The administrator also sees the indicators alert.
  • Members: a list with search and filters, invitations, access requests and coach-change requests, and each person’s record.

Operations: report engine, emails and alerts

AI report engine

Corex does not use Telegram bots. Its analysis engine is a chain of five AI agents:

#AgentWhat it does
1Anthropometric interpreterComposition, somatotype, proportionality, risk and health
2Strength and conditioning coachTraining approaches, with no sets, reps, loads or timelines
3Sports nutrition advisorQualitative guidance, with no calories, grams or supplements; ends by stating that the plan is set by a professional
4Verifier, on a different modelChecks that nothing contradicts the numbers or identifies the person, and removes prescriptions, made-up facts, generic phrases and claims about anything not measured
5FormatterArranges the verified text into the seven sections
  • It only receives anonymized data: sex, age, weight, height and the calculation. Never name, email, ID number, routine or plan. Anything missing is marked as unavailable, with an instruction not to infer it.
  • The coach’s notes travel as data, not as instructions: they are stripped of personal data and wrapped in markers that change with every request, and if the response copies them, it is rejected.
  • Style: every statement is anchored to a data point, inferences are labelled as such, and the report states that it is general guidance, not a professional’s judgement.
  • Failures: if a section is missing, the report is marked as an error and never saved half-done; the coach sees a clear message and the technical detail stays in the logs. If the main model fails, it retries and falls back to a backup model, and the report shows which model answered for each agent.
  • Limits: rate limits in the app and in the engine, and only one report generating per assessment.

Indicators (administrator dashboard)

  • By service: database size, scheduled jobs, system errors by module, AI reports stuck or in error, backup and restore test, and usage of external services.
  • Operational: access requests awaiting approval and pending coach changes, with the age of the oldest; incomplete profiles; people.
  • Each card has a status (critical, attention, OK) with thresholds, and demo people are not counted.

Controls

  • Access: rules in the database on every table, switched on automatically for new tables; protected columns (the coach’s notes and the person’s sensitive data); every server action identifies the person from their session, and a change-control rule checks this; and the screen never offers what the server will reject.
  • Four eyes: whoever requests a coach change cannot resolve it.
  • Concurrent editing: a per-athlete lock that the server enforces on every write, and conflict detection if someone else saved in the meantime.
  • Web security: protection against forged requests, a content security policy and security headers; sign-in and sign-up never reveal whether an account exists; validation in three layers (screen, server and database) with the same limits; exports protected against formula injection; and rate limits per action.
  • Personal data: data-processing consent is required at sign-up and when completing the profile, and cannot be modified or deleted; AI consent is given per report; and data is anonymized before anything is sent to the AI.
  • Human in the loop: the athlete sees no report until the coach publishes it, and the database enforces this.
  • Audit: the activity log is written by the server; the administrator, an analyst with permission or the person themselves can read it, and it is kept for a limited time.
  • Change control: the same checks on every commit and in continuous integration (repository rules, types, linting, tests and build); pushes to the main branch are blocked if the last integration run failed.
  • Backups: daily and encrypted, with a row count per table; a weekly restore test in a separate environment; the result is logged without personal data.

Operational details

  • Measurements: basic measurements, 8 skinfolds, 6 girths and 3 breadths; up to 3 readings per measurement (mean of 2 or median of 3); a third is requested if the difference exceeds 5% for skinfolds or 1% for the rest.
  • Formulas: body-fat % by Faulkner and Carter; muscle mass by Lee (2000), bone mass by Rocha (1975) and fat mass by Kerr (1988); Z-scores with the Phantom model.
  • Nutrition: resting expenditure with Cunningham (when there is an ISAK assessment) or Mifflin-St Jeor; activity factors from 1.2 to 1.9; target calories between −25% and +20%; protein from 1.8 to 2.2 g/kg and carbohydrates from 3 to 10 g/kg; fat fills the rest, between 20% and 35%; hydration of 35 ml/kg plus extra for activity.
  • Report models: main gemini-3.1-flash-lite, backup gemini-2.5-flash-lite and verifier gemini-2.5-flash; temperature 0.4 for the first three agents, 0 for the verifier and 0.3 for the formatter.
  • Report engine: 2 attempts per model with increasing backoff and 5 seconds between agents; the app waits up to 15 seconds for the engine to accept the job and offers a retry after 7 minutes; limits of 5 requests per minute in the app and 20 per minute per IP in the engine; coach’s notes up to 2,000 characters; only Z-scores beyond ±2 are sent; the verifier applies 8 rules.
  • Sign-in: 6-digit code, valid for 10 minutes; minimum response time of 1.5 seconds; status notices at most once every 10 minutes.
  • Editing: the lock expires after 2 minutes without a heartbeat or 15 without changes; the draft is saved 2 seconds after each change and expires after 14 days.
  • Activity log: 12 months; purged on the 1st of each month.
  • Backup: at 03:17, Colombia time; restore test on Sundays; kept encrypted for 30 days.
  • Catalogs: 366 items of 34 types, 94 foods and 100 exercises across 14 muscle groups.
