Tanteo

Sales, cash and expenses for micro-businesses that are not yet required to issue electronic invoices.

It records every sale at the counter with an internal receipt, closes the day's cash with a count, records expenses by reading the QR code on suppliers' electronic invoices, and warns when the business is approaching the threshold that would require it to issue invoices.

Status
In production · pilot business
Period
Aug 2026 – present
Role
Independent project, built for friends’ businesses

What it is

Tanteo replaces the spreadsheet of a neighbourhood business (a convenience store, a café, a small shop) owned by an individual who is not yet required to issue electronic invoices.

  • The counter sells with a barcode scanner and issues an internal sales receipt, which is not a tax invoice.
  • The cash is closed every night.
  • Expenses come in by reading the QR code on the supplier's invoice.
  • The owner sees the month's result and their accountant downloads what they need.
  • One platform serves several businesses, each with its data kept separate.

Who uses it and what each one sees

  • Owner: sees the whole business. Only the owner can void receipts, reopen a closed day, correct or void expenses, edit the catalog, invite the team and link the bot.
  • Cashier: sells, opens and closes the till and registers customers. Sees only the day's receipts and expenses, and cannot void or reopen.
  • Accountant: read-only access to receipts, expenses, reports and exports, for any date.
  • End customer: needs no account; signs up with a QR code printed at the counter and gets the receipt printed or by email.
  • Platform team: cannot see a real business's data, only its identity and status, never amounts, customers or sales. It can only enter test businesses, and every lookup is audited.

What it records

  • Sales receipt: gap-free sequential number, cashier, customer (a generic walk-in customer by default), lines, taxes, discounts and one or more payments. Prices and taxes are frozen at the time of sale. Statuses: issued → voided, only by the owner, with a reason and while the day is open. Never deleted.
  • Day close: opening balance per account, cash counted note by note, transfers to the main cash box, withdrawals, expected versus counted, and the difference. Statuses: open → closed; only the owner reopens it, with a reason.
  • Expense: supplier, date, which account the money came from, category (supplies, fixed costs, services, payroll or other) and taxes. It can be corrected or voided only while the day is open.
  • Catalog: products with barcodes, taxes and discounts (percentage, fixed amount, open or "buy X, pay Y"), automatic promotions by weekday or date, and supplies and recipes: every sale draws down inventory.
  • Customers and suppliers: validated ID, contact details and data consent. Deleting someone anonymizes them.

How it works

  1. Selling at the counter

    The cashier picks the customer, scans the products (or uses quick buttons), applies discounts and takes payment into one account or as a split payment, with a change calculator. The server takes prices and taxes from the catalog, not from the screen, so nobody can alter them. Open orders let the cashier keep several sales going at once for customers still in the shop.

  2. Offline

    Every sale is saved on the device first and sent in order when the connection returns, without duplicates; the day close waits until everything has been uploaded.

  3. Day close

    Open the till, review sales by account, count the cash, record transfers and expenses, and close. Once the day is closed, no sales, expenses or voids get in.

  4. Expenses

    Scan the QR code on the supplier's electronic invoice, upload its file or type it in. The supplier is created if it does not exist yet, and an invoice that is already recorded is rejected.

  5. Catalog

    Edited on screen or imported from a spreadsheet in a single operation that is cancelled entirely if anything fails. It prints a barcode sheet.

  6. Customer sign-up

    By QR code, with bot protection. The response is the same if the person was already registered, so nobody can find out who is a customer.

  7. Team

    The owner invites people with a link valid for a single email address.

  8. Results

    The month's result (sales, expenses and operating profit), the main cash box and the best-selling products; exports for the accountant's accounting software; and a formalization indicator that warns when the year's revenue is approaching the threshold for mandatory e-invoicing.

Screens

Controls

  • Separation between businesses: every rule lives in the database; keys prevent a record from pointing to another business; money operations go in only through server functions; and tests confirm that another business's owner sees zero rows that are not theirs.
  • The platform cannot see a real business: no rule grants it access, the dashboard receives masked ID numbers, and metrics are counts, with no amounts.
  • Least privilege: the probe and the demo use real cashier or owner sessions, not the master key.
  • Integrity: immutable, gap-free receipts; data frozen at the time of sale; no sale or expense is ever duplicated; and a closed day stays untouched unless it is reopened with an audit trail.
  • Audit: every relevant action is logged; the owner, the accountant and the platform can see it.
  • Personal data: consent is required, from the owner when creating the business and from the customer at sign-up; deletion anonymizes the record.
  • Change control: the same checks on every commit, every push and in continuous integration; dependency and secret scanning; the access matrix as a non-negotiable gate; and a smoke test after every deployment.

Integrations

  • Supabase: database, sign-in with a code and functions.
  • Cloudflare: website, the worker for the bot and probes, encrypted backups and bot protection.
  • Telegram: the bot.
  • Email: sign-in codes and critical alerts.
  • GitHub Actions: checks, watchdog, backup and demo.
  • Accounting software: file export only.

  • Cloudflare Workers, Pages and R2

Figures

  • 39 tables, all with access policies (52 policies); 111 functions, 13 views and 34 triggers; 3 Edge Functions.
  • 679 access-test assertions, about 396 unit tests and 66 end-to-end tests; 23 change-control rules; 150 SQL patches applied.

Operational details

  • Owner summary: at 21:00, Colombia time.
  • Canary: every 5 minutes from 06:00 to 21:55, and every 30 minutes overnight.
  • Alerts: critical ones repeat every hour; the rest every 24 hours.
  • Backup: at 03:00, with the last 90 kept. Watchdog: hourly. Database jobs: 06:00 and 06:30.
  • Invitation: valid for 7 days. Bot code: 6 digits, 10 minutes and 5 attempts.
  • Import: up to 2,000 rows. Offline sales: accepted up to 7 days back.
  • Indicator: the legal revenue threshold (3,500 UVT in Colombia), with warnings at 70% and 90%.
